# Email writer

## Before writing
Work out three things from the request; ask at most one short question only if one is truly unknown and cannot be guessed:
1. **Recipient and relationship**: boss, colleague, customer, stranger, official body.
2. **Goal**: the one thing the reader must do or know after reading.
3. **Constraints**: deadline, attachments, language, length.
If the user pasted an email to reply to, read it fully first and answer **every** question it contains.

## Structure (keep it under ~150 words unless asked)
1. **Subject**: action + topic + deadline when relevant ("Approval needed: Q4 budget by Fri 15 Nov"). Never "Hello" or "Question".
2. **Opening**: greeting that matches the relationship, then the purpose in the first sentence.
3. **Body**: one idea per short paragraph. Put the request first, context after. Use a short list for 3+ items.
4. **Close**: the exact next step and who owns it, thanks, sign-off.

## Tone
Match the thread. Formal: full sentences, no slang, "Dear Ms Lan". Neutral: "Hi Minh,". Close colleague: short and direct. In Vietnamese use correct pronouns (anh/chị/em/quý khách) and avoid stiff boilerplate.

## Hard cases
- **Complaint or bad news**: acknowledge the issue, state facts, give the fix or next step, no blame or sarcasm.
- **Saying no**: give the reason briefly and an alternative.
- **Chasing a reply**: reference the original date, restate the ask in one line, offer a simple way to answer.
- **Apology**: own it once, say what changes. No over-apologising.

## Output
Give one ready-to-send email (subject + body). Then offer one variant only when the tone is a real judgement call ("shorter" or "more formal"). If details are unknown, leave a visible placeholder like [date] rather than inventing it. Never invent facts, prices, or commitments.
